# Reading list

Sources and further reading behind the foundational document, grouped by tradition, with notes on what the text draws from and where to read it. Where a free edition exists it is linked first, because a document that costs nothing should not require an expensive shelf. Modern editions are suggestions, not requirements.

The document reads these sources as ancestors and witnesses: with gratitude, without obedience. A citation identifies a source; it does not make the source an authority, and repeated ideas across traditions are not treated as proof. English wording varies by translation, so readers should check the cited work and passage rather than treating one rendering as uniquely canonical.

Links point to free, lawful editions: public-domain texts, or sites that publish their translations for free reading. Links are checked automatically; if one breaks, a pull request fixing it counts as a source correction and can be merged immediately.

## Start here

If you read only six things, read these. Together they cover most of what the document says, in the ancestors' own words.

1. Viktor Frankl, *Man's Search for Meaning*.
2. The Bhagavad Gita, chapters 2 and 6.
3. The Dhammapada, and the Kalama Sutta.
4. The Sermon on the Mount, Matthew 5 to 7.
5. Epictetus, *Enchiridion*.
6. William James, *The Varieties of Religious Experience*, lectures I, XVI, and XVII.

## Where the free texts live

Most citations resolve to a handful of sites. Once you know the pattern, you can go straight to any verse.

- [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/): the Hebrew Bible, Mishnah, and Talmud, with the public-domain 1917 JPS translation among others. Pattern: `sefaria.org/Genesis.1.27`. Sefaria shows Hebrew verse numbering.
- [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/) in the King James Version, which is public domain in the United States. Pattern: `biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+5:37&version=KJV`. The whole KJV is also [Project Gutenberg #10](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10), and the public-domain World English Bible is at [ebible.org](https://ebible.org/).
- [Quran.com](https://quran.com/): every surah with many translations. Pattern: `quran.com/47/19`.
- [Sunnah.com](https://sunnah.com/): hadith with Arabic, English, and grading. Pattern: `sunnah.com/bukhari:13`.
- [SuttaCentral](https://suttacentral.net/): the Pali discourses in Bhikkhu Sujato's public-domain translations. Pattern: `suttacentral.net/mn22/en/sujato`.
- [Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/): the Analects and Tao Te Ching in Legge's translation beside the Chinese.
- [Sacred Texts](https://www.sacred-texts.com/): public-domain translations of the Vedas, Upanishads, Mahabharata, Jain sutras, and more.
- [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/): public-domain books. Pattern: `gutenberg.org/ebooks/NUMBER`.
- [SearchGurbani](https://www.searchgurbani.com/) and [SriGranth](https://www.srigranth.org/): the Guru Granth Sahib by Ang, and the Dasam Granth.
- [Bahá'í Reference Library](https://www.bahai.org/library/): the Bahá'í writings.
- [MIT Internet Classics Archive](http://classics.mit.edu/): Greek and Roman philosophy in older public-domain translations.

## Hindu texts and the epics

- **Rig Veda** 1.164.46 (one reality named in many ways by the wise).
  Free: [Griffith's 1896 translation, hymn 1.164](https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/rigveda/rv01164.htm), dated but complete. Modern: Jamison and Brereton (Oxford), the scholarly standard.
- **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** 4.5.15 (neti, neti; the phrase also recurs at 2.3.6 and 3.9.26). **Katha Upanishad** 2.3.10 (the highest state).
  Free: [Max Müller's translation, Sacred Books of the East vol. 15](https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/sbe15/), which contains both. Modern: Patrick Olivelle (Oxford World's Classics) for accuracy; Eknath Easwaran for readability.
- **Bhagavad Gita** 2:14, 2:20, 2:31-38, 2:47, 6:5.
  Free: Edwin Arnold's *The Song Celestial* (1885), [Project Gutenberg #2388](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2388). Modern: Eknath Easwaran; Laurie Patton (Penguin Classics); Winthrop Sargeant for a word-by-word edition.
- **Mahabharata**, Anushasana Parva 113 in Ganguli's numbering, 13.114.8 in the critical edition (do nothing to another that you would regard as injurious to yourself). *Ahimsa paramo dharmah*, "non-violence is the highest duty," is a recurring Mahabharata formula later adopted as a Jain watchword; it is not a saying of Mahavira.
  Free: [Kisari Mohan Ganguli's complete translation](https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/) (1883-1896); the cited passage is in Anushasana Parva, section CXIII.
- **Yoga Sutras of Patanjali** (dhyana). Useful comparative reading for concentrated contemplative states, although the optional deeper-sitting appendix does not present them as a direct source for its method.
  Modern: Edwin Bryant's translation with commentary (North Point Press).

## Buddhist texts

- **Dhammapada** 5, 21, 129-130, 277.
  Free: Bhikkhu Sujato's translation on SuttaCentral: [verses 1-20](https://suttacentral.net/dhp1-20/en/sujato), [21-32](https://suttacentral.net/dhp21-32/en/sujato), [129-145](https://suttacentral.net/dhp129-145/en/sujato), [273-289](https://suttacentral.net/dhp273-289/en/sujato). Also [Acharya Buddharakkhita's translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/index.html) at Access to Insight and [Max Müller's](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2017) at Project Gutenberg. Modern: Gil Fronsdal (Shambhala).
- **Kalama Sutta**, [Anguttara Nikaya 3.65](https://suttacentral.net/an3.65/en/sujato) (do not go by hearsay, tradition, or scripture). **Alagaddupama Sutta**, [Majjhima Nikaya 22](https://suttacentral.net/mn22/en/sujato) (the raft). **Mahasaccaka Sutta**, [Majjhima Nikaya 36](https://suttacentral.net/mn36/en/sujato) (the austerities and the middle way). **Mahaparinibbana Sutta**, [Digha Nikaya 16](https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato) (be lamps, or islands, unto yourselves; strive on with diligence). The four noble truths, **Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta**, [Samyutta Nikaya 56.11](https://suttacentral.net/sn56.11/en/sujato). The **Vinaya**, the monastic code, cited for its graduated procedures, is in the Vinaya section of SuttaCentral.
  Modern print: Bhikkhu Bodhi's translations of the Majjhima and Anguttara Nikayas (Wisdom Publications).
- **Udanavarga** 5:18. The golden-rule line commonly attributed to this verse, "Hurt not others in ways that you yourself would find hurtful," cannot be securely traced to that verse; the document therefore leads with the Dhammapada 129-130 parallel.
  Free: W. W. Rockhill's 1883 translation from the Tibetan, *Udanavarga: A Collection of Verses from the Buddhist Canon*, on [the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/search?query=Rockhill+Udanavarga).
- **Dogen**, *Tenzo Kyokun* (Instructions for the Cook). Dogen instructs the cook to care for the rice, vegetables, and other ingredients as for his own eyes, quoting the Chan master Baoning Renyong.
  Free: [a full translation](https://wwzc.org/tenzo-kyokun-instructions-tenzo/) published by the White Wind Zen Community. Modern: *From the Zen Kitchen to Enlightenment* (Uchiyama and Wright, Weatherhill), or Kazuaki Tanahashi's *Moon in a Dewdrop*.
- **Thich Nhat Hanh**, *The Miracle of Mindfulness* (Beacon Press) for washing the dishes; *The Heart of Understanding* (Parallax Press) for interbeing and the cloud in the paper. Under copyright; see "Borrowing the modern books" below.

## Jain texts

- **Acaranga Sutra** 1.2.3 (all beings are fond of life and dislike pain) and 1.4.1 (all breathing, existing, living beings should not be slain). **Anekantavada**, the doctrine of many-sidedness.
  Free: [Hermann Jacobi's translation, Sacred Books of the East vol. 22](https://www.sacred-texts.com/jai/sbe22/).

## Sikh texts

- **Guru Granth Sahib**: Ik Onkar, the opening word; [Ang 62](https://www.searchgurbani.com/guru-granth-sahib/ang/62) (truth is high, higher still is truthful living). **Guru Nanak**: the three pillars (naam japna, kirat karni, vand chakna), a traditional summary of his teaching rather than a scriptural list; the langar; and "there is no Hindu, there is no Muslim," preserved in the Janamsakhi traditions rather than in scripture. The principle of the sangat, that the Guru is present in the assembled community, is a teaching of the tradition rather than a single quotable verse.
  Free: [SearchGurbani](https://www.searchgurbani.com/) and [SriGranth](https://www.srigranth.org/), with Sant Singh Khalsa's English translation, searchable by Ang.
- **Guru Gobind Singh**, Akal Ustat, verse 85 (recognize the whole human race as one), in the Dasam Granth.
  Free: the Dasam Granth on [SearchGurbani](https://www.searchgurbani.com/).

## Zoroastrian texts

- **Yasna** 30:2, from the Gathas (hear with your ears the best things; each one for himself choose). The triad *humata, hukhta, hvarshta*, good thoughts, good words, good deeds, comes from the Avestan liturgy (for example Yasna 35.2, in the Yasna Haptanghaiti), not from the Gathas themselves.
  Free: [avesta.org](https://www.avesta.org/) has the Gathas in several translations. Modern: Stanley Insler, *The Gathas of Zarathustra* (1975), the scholarly standard.

## Sufi and bhakti poets

- **Rumi**, *Masnavi* I:678-683: ten lamps in one place differ in form, yet their light cannot be told apart; in things of the spirit there is no division and no number. The popular line "The lamps are different, but the Light is the same," often extended with "one matter, one energy, one Light-mind," is a modern paraphrase and is not Nicholson's wording.
  Free: [Nicholson's translation of the passage, line by line with the Persian](https://masnavi.net/3/25/eng/1/678/), at masnavi.net. Modern: Jawid Mojaddedi (Oxford World's Classics) or Alan Williams (Penguin Classics), Book One.
- **Kabir** (where do you search for me, friend?).
  Free: *Songs of Kabir*, translated by Rabindranath Tagore (1915), [Project Gutenberg #6519](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6519). Tagore worked from a collection whose authenticity scholars dispute. Modern: Arvind Krishna Mehrotra, *Songs of Kabir* (NYRB Classics), from better-attested sources.

## Indigenous traditions

- The **Haudenosaunee** Great Law of Peace and later seventh-generation formulations are relevant to the stewardship discussion. The familiar wording is modern and does not appear verbatim in the Great Law.
  Readers should prefer Haudenosaunee sources, beginning with the [Haudenosaunee Confederacy](https://www.haudenosauneeconfederacy.com/), when tracing the principle.
- For historical context on religious peyote use, see Omer Stewart, *Peyote Religion: A History* (University of Oklahoma Press). Appendix B mentions long-standing psychoactive sacramental traditions only as context; it does not make their practices part of the table's required practice.

## Modern witnesses

- **Viktor Frankl**, *Man's Search for Meaning* (Beacon Press). The three roads to meaning, the last freedom, the rule of living as if for the second time, and tragic optimism are all here; tragic optimism is in the postscript added in 1984. The line that happiness and success "cannot be pursued" but "must ensue" is in Frankl's preface to later editions; it is said of happiness and success, not of meaning.
- **Etty Hillesum**, *An Interrupted Life: The Diaries, 1941-1943, and Letters from Westerbork* (Henry Holt).
- **Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn**, *The Gulag Archipelago* (the line dividing good and evil). The passage is in Part IV, chapter 1, "The Ascent." The abridged edition (Harper Perennial) includes it.
- **Albert Camus**, *The Plague* and *The Myth of Sisyphus* (Vintage International).
- **Friedrich Nietzsche**, *Twilight of the Idols*, "Maxims and Arrows" 12 (he who has a why to live for can bear almost any how). Frankl quotes it.
  Free: Ludovici's translation, [Project Gutenberg #52263](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52263), where the section is titled "Maxims and Missiles."

## Consciousness and psychology

- **William James**, *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902). By their fruits, not their roots, is in Lecture I; the authority of mystical states is in Lectures XVI and XVII.
  Free: [Project Gutenberg #621](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/621).
- **Robert Monroe**, *Journeys Out of the Body* (1971), *Far Journeys* (1985), *Ultimate Journey* (1994), all Doubleday. - **Carl Rogers**, *On Becoming a Person* (Houghton Mifflin), for unconditional positive regard and being heard without judgment.
- **Ramana Maharshi**, *Nan Yar?* (Who Am I?), the short text on self-inquiry.
  Free: published by Sri Ramanasramam at [sriramanamaharshi.org](https://www.sriramanamaharshi.org/).
- **Jiddu Krishnamurti**, the speech dissolving the Order of the Star, Ommen, 3 August 1929 (truth is a pathless land).
  Free: the full text at [jkrishnamurti.org](https://jkrishnamurti.org/).

## A note on verification

References are provided so readers can check the document's claims against their sources. English wording varies by edition and translation, and verse numbering differs between Hebrew and English Bibles and between editions of the Analects. Some traditional sayings circulate in forms whose textual history is uncertain. Where the foundational document uses a paraphrase rather than a stable quotation, this list aims to identify the underlying source or tradition rather than manufacture false precision.

Particular care is warranted with widely circulated attributions: the Udanavarga golden rule, popular Rumi lines, Tagore's Kabir, and modern renderings of the Haudenosaunee seventh-generation principle. Corrections, stronger primary-source references, and better translation notes are welcome by pull request. No entry in this file should be read as a claim that every wording has a single uncontested English form.
